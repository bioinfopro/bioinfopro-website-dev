# About us

The Association of Bioinformatics Professionals sets skill-based standards for the practice of bioinformatics and builds the community around them. We're for the people who do this work — scientists who combine code and biology to move life sciences forward, however they learned to do it, whatever their degree says. Founded in Toronto in 2026, we exist for practitioners across the field, not one background or career path.

Wherever you entered bioinformatics from, there's a place for you here.

## Why we exist

Bioinformatics grew out of academic labs, without anyone ever stopping to define the job. There's still no formal scope of practice and no professional body to set one, so when employers need to judge skill, most fall back on academic credentials instead. That filters out most of the field: 89% of the computational biology workforce is self-taught or trained outside a formal degree program.

Doctoral training compounds the gap. Graduate programs are built to produce academic researchers who publish fast, not practitioners who write reproducible, collaborative, industry-ready code. Graduates reach the workforce ready for one set of expectations and meet another — a mismatch in the pipeline, not a failing of the graduates or the mentors who trained them.

That gap is why we exist. Nobody had defined bioinformatics as its own profession, so we did.
